WORKING DEFINITIONS

A shared language for the case.

Original creditor
The organization where the account originated.
Current debt owner
The party recorded as holding the debt now; distinct from its collection agent.
Collector
An organization assigned to handle collection communication for an account.
Routing history
A record of the organizations assigned to an account over time.
Proposal
Recorded settlement terms awaiting the appropriate review and acceptance.
Offer revision
A distinct version of proposed terms that preserves the earlier version.
Expiration date
The date through which a particular proposal is recorded as available.
Acceptance
A separate record that a specific party agreed to a specific revision.
Installment schedule
Planned payment dates and amounts; not evidence of cleared payments.
Funding assumption
An input used for a hypothetical plan that has not been independently verified.
Local hold
A conservative planning reservation used in the prototype’s scenario.
Reconciliation
Matching recorded activity to supporting provider evidence.
Company tenant
A company’s scoped workspace and records within the product architecture.
Shared document
A file deliberately made available within a defined access scope.
State review
A company-specific eligibility and service-scope review supported by evidence.
Audit record
A record of an action and its context, distinct from the action’s business outcome.
01 / The work

Use the same words for the same record.

Clear terminology matters when sales, negotiations, servicing and management share a case. These are product workflow definitions, not legal definitions or advice about a particular consumer debt.

02 / The process

Keep the language as clear as the process.

  1. Original creditor: the organization associated with the source account. Current owner: the organization recorded as holding the debt now. Collector or servicer: the organization handling the account conversation. These roles may differ.
  2. Offer: proposed terms for a specific debt. Revision: a distinct version of those terms. Acceptance evidence: a verified record tied to the exact terms presented, not a staff note or creditor reply.
  3. Installment: a scheduled obligation. Verified payment: a provider-backed payment event. Reversal: an event reducing previously recorded funds. A hypothetical hold: a planning reservation that is not a money transfer.
03 / Context & decisions

The details that matter.

A tenant is a customer company boundary within the planned SettleLift product project. An office is a location or operating unit inside that company. A platform administrator operates the software platform and is distinct from a customer’s business owner. An outbox records a delivery job; it does not itself prove a message was delivered. These distinctions are used throughout the website and prepared schema.

05 / Common question

Why distinguish a proposal from a completed settlement?

Because recording an amount does not establish consumer acceptance, payment or discharge of the debt. The workflow needs separate evidence at each relevant step.
