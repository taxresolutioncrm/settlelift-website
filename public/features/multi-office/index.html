01 / The work

One product. Clear company boundaries.

A customer owns its debt settlement business; RomyLabs operates the CRM platform. That distinction should remain clear in branding, administration and permissions. The planned architecture uses a dedicated SettleLift product database with isolated company tenants and office records inside each company.

02 / The process

How the work connects.

  1. Create the company identity and office structure. Keep customer business ownership separate from the protected platform administrator mapping.
  2. Assign staff roles and case responsibilities. A user’s editable profile should never grant access to another office or promote the account to platform administrator.
  3. Review the company’s state service scope and provider configuration. Invite staff and clients only after the tenant’s actual authentication and storage boundaries are verified.
03 / Context & decisions

The details that matter.

The prepared schema stores authorization in private membership and assignment tables. Composite relationships keep child records in the correct tenant, and financial evidence is not directly writable by ordinary staff. Local tests exercise owners, assigned users, outsiders and the protected platform role. These checks do not replace production Supabase testing, advisor review or a certified provisioning and invitation flow.

05 / Common question

Does each office need its own CRM product database?

The planned model is one dedicated Supabase project for SettleLift, with isolated customer companies and their offices inside it. Other RomyLabs CRM products remain separate. Real provisioning is not active yet.
